PRODUCT

Croonbox is an asynchronous video interview platform for hiring teams.

The core idea is simple:

An employer creates a job and defines the interview they want candidates to complete.

They add structured interview questions and configure things such as preparation time, response limits, retakes, candidate instructions and interview deadline.

They publish the interview and invite candidates.

Invitations can be sent individually, to multiple candidates, or by importing a CSV. Resend will handle transactional email.

Candidates do not create Croonbox accounts.

Each candidate receives a secure interview link.

When they open it, they should have a polished candidate experience that explains the interview, checks their camera and microphone, and guides them through the questions one at a time.

For each question the candidate gets preparation time, records their video answer, can preview/retake where allowed, submits the response and continues.

The interview should survive reasonable interruptions. If a candidate leaves after completing some questions and returns through their link, their completed work should still be there and they should continue from where they stopped.

Candidate videos should be stored privately using Supabase Storage.

After the candidate submits the full interview, Croonbox processes their responses.

Each response is transcribed.

AI then analyzes what the candidate actually said in the context of the job, question and any evaluation guidance provided by the employer.

We are deliberately NOT building numerical candidate scoring.

Do not produce things like:
83%
8/10
confidence scores
personality scores
facial analysis
emotion analysis
candidate rankings

AI should instead produce useful qualitative hiring insights such as:

- answer summary
- relevant evidence from the answer
- strong signals
- areas worth reviewing
- overall interview summary
- relevant experience demonstrated
- areas to explore further
- useful human follow-up questions

The hiring team makes the decision.

The product should feel operational rather than like a collection of demo dashboards.

The primary employer journey is:

Create → Invite → Review → Decide.

CANDIDATE EXPERIENCE

The candidate side should be extremely simple.

They should never feel like they have entered another complicated SaaS platform.

Their experience is:

Open interview link
→ understand the interview
→ check camera/microphone
→ answer questions
→ submit
→ done.

Design this experience particularly well for mobile because candidates may complete interviews from their phones.

SAAS STRUCTURE

Croonbox is multi-tenant.

Every company has its own workspace and its data must remain isolated from every other company.

There are ordinary employer users, organization owners/team members, candidates using secure interview links, and a private Croonbox platform administrator.

Use a simple Owner / Member organization permission model for V1 rather than overengineering permissions.

There should also be a private platform-admin area that allows the Croonbox operator to understand what is happening across the SaaS: organizations, users, jobs, subscriptions, interview activity, usage, processing problems, email problems, etc.

TECHNOLOGY

Use Next.js App Router.

Use Supabase Postgres as the database.

Do NOT use Supabase Auth.

Implement normal application authentication with secure password storage and sessions, with signup, login, logout and password reset.

Use Supabase Storage for private interview video storage.

I can manually create required Supabase buckets if necessary.

Use Resend for transactional emails.

Use Stripe for billing/subscriptions.

Use OpenAI for transcription and qualitative interview insights.

The client currently has their domain and hosting associated with cPanel, but we have not inspected that hosting yet.

Do not architect the application around cPanel.

Keep the application deployment-portable. When we eventually inspect the cPanel environment, we will decide whether the Node application can run there or whether the domain should simply point to another application host.

Configuration, service keys, domains, Stripe IDs, Resend settings, bucket names, model names and other environment-specific values should live in .env/environment configuration rather than being scattered through the codebase.

DATA / DOMAIN THINKING

Reason properly about the underlying domain instead of modeling everything around pages.

Conceptually the system revolves around:

organizations
users/memberships
jobs
interview questions
candidates
candidate invitations
interview sessions
candidate responses
video assets
transcripts
AI insights
human reviews/notes
subscriptions/usage
emails
activity
processing state

A candidate identity should not necessarily be the same thing as their participation in one job because the same person could potentially be invited to another interview later.

Think carefully about state transitions and failure cases.

For example:

candidate invited
→ interview started
→ responses progressively saved
→ interview completed
→ processing
→ ready for review
→ human reviewed

Handle real-world cases such as interrupted interviews, failed uploads, failed transcription, AI processing failure, duplicate webhooks, invalid invite links, plan limits and unauthorized cross-organization access.

Do not silently lose user work.
